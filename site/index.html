Most sites are mapped in one person's head.

New staff learn the layout by guessing. Runners lose minutes hunting for a car, a key box or the right entrance while guests wait. And every property you operate does it a little differently.

Training
Learned by shadowing, shift after shift.
Retrieval
Slow when the one person who knows is off shift.
Standards

From a sketch on paper to every phone on your team.

Most managers already draw their site by hand. Start there.

  1. 1 · Draw

    Start from your sketch

    Snap a photo of your drawing and trace over it, or answer four questions and let the builder lay out the whole site.

  2. 2 · Publish

    Publish it to your team

    Invite managers and attendants by email or a six-character site code. Edits stay in draft until you publish.

  3. 3 · Find

    Find anything

    Your team types a space number or a landmark. It lights up on the right floor. It works in any phone's browser, with nothing to install.

Six levels. One search.

Every floor gets its own color, the same way real garages sign their levels. Ramps, stairs and elevators connect them, and a search opens the map on the right floor.

Built for the people who run the lot, and simple enough for their first day.
